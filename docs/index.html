Accent

What this is

One accent hue in, a whole token set out, for both schemes. Everything on this page is drawn with the tokens shown below it, so what you see is the output and not a picture of it. Pick a hue in the bar — including one no product uses yet — and the planes, the three text weights, the accent family and the danger family are all re-derived, contrast-solved, and re-checked live.

Planes

Each plane sits on the one before it. The hue is the product's own, held at a very low chroma, so the neutrals read as one material rather than as grey with a cast dropped over it.

Text

--text is a fixed point on the ramp. --text-dim and --text-faint are solved against --surface-2 — the fill of every field, and the tightest ground either really sits on. Solving against --bg instead is what once shipped a --text-faint that measured 3.92:1 where it was checked and 3.18:1 where it was printed.

Accent and danger

--accent is exactly what the product declared and is never adjusted. The other four follow from it. --accent-ink tries both near-black and near-white and keeps whichever wins on that hue — hardcoding one is how a delete-everything button shipped at 2.48:1.

Contrast audit

Every pairing the token table promises will clear AA, measured on the values above. build.js runs this same check and refuses to write a file that fails, so a ratio can only regress if somebody hand-edits generated CSS.

Buttons

Three tiers, no more. Primary takes the accent fill and there is one per view. Normal is --surface-2. Quiet is transparent. Destructive is not a fourth tier but a colour applied to a quiet or normal button — only the confirm button of a destructive dialog is filled.

Tiers

Fields

A field is a fill, not a hole. At rest it is --surface-2 with a transparent border; on focus the fill lifts to --surface, the border takes the accent, and the ring appears. font: inherit, always — a sentence typed in must look like the same sentence read back out.

Chips

Filters are pills, actions are boxes. A chip changes what is shown and never what is stored. The count sits at opacity: .55 in tabular numerals; the selected one takes the accent tint, never the full fill — the full fill belongs to the primary action alone.

Rows and the overflow menu

A row shows its content and one ⋯. What it says about itself may be clickable, but everything you can do to it lives behind that one trigger — and never behind hover, because these products are used on touch screens. Destructive rows sit last.

Messages and empty states

Three kinds of message, three treatments. Progress belongs to the control that started it. Outcome stays until something replaces it and is never auto-dismissed if it names a failure or a number. Aside acknowledges something already visible and may clear itself. An empty list teaches; a filtered-empty is a different, shorter sentence.
